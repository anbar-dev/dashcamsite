Parking mode: what do you need when the engine is off?

A dash cam can record while your car is parked, but it needs power and suitable settings. Here’s what to check before choosing a camera and kit.

Parking mode records while the car is stationary. Depending on the model, it may save events detected by a G-sensor, detect motion, keep buffered footage from before an event, or continue recording in time-lapse or low-bitrate mode. Exact options vary by model and firmware.

The practical rule: “supports parking mode” does not mean the dash cam can stay on with the engine off using only a USB cable connected to the cigarette lighter socket.

Check these five things

1. Power with the car off

Check the manual for how to power the model while parked. Many installations need a compatible hardwire kit connected to the fuse box or a dedicated battery pack. Some cars switch off the 12V socket when the engine is off.

2. Battery protection

A suitable hardwire kit can cut power below a configurable voltage threshold or after a set time. Choose settings according to the manual, battery type and condition, and how you use the car. Continuous recording can still affect the battery; no kit removes that risk in every situation.

3. Exact compatibility

Check the kit’s model number, connector, supported voltage, and camera compatibility. Don’t rely on the product family name alone: cameras from the same brand can use different accessories. Also check whether the kit is included in the bundle being sold.

4. The right memory card

Parking mode keeps writing to the microSD card. Use the capacity and speed class recommended by the manufacturer, and consider a high-endurance card for frequent overwriting. Three-channel footage uses more storage than a single camera.

5. Safe installation

Route cables away from curtain airbags, pedals, sightlines, and moving parts. If you’re not familiar with fuse wiring or removing interior trim, use a qualified installer. Don’t connect a camera to a circuit you haven’t identified.

Which recording mode should you choose?

  • Buffered event detection: keeps footage from around a detected impact or movement; check how many seconds before the event are saved.
  • Time-lapse: saves frames at intervals, reducing frame count and storage use.
  • Low bitrate: records continuously in smaller files; check the resulting image quality and power use.
  • Collision-triggered clips: begin after the camera senses an impact. They may miss light contact that does not trigger the G-sensor.

Feature names are not consistent across brands. Read the manual for your exact model and firmware, and test the setup somewhere safe before relying on it.

Pre-purchase checklist

  • Is the listing the exact channel count and model suffix you want?
  • Which compatible kit powers this specific camera with the engine off?
  • Is the kit included, or sold separately?
  • What cutoff voltage and timer can you set, and how do they match your vehicle battery?
  • How much card capacity do the camera count and recording mode require?
  • Will the rear camera cable reach your back window?
  • Can you route wires without interfering with airbags, sightlines, or vehicle wiring?
