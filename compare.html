Video quality, night driving, and license plates

Resolution describes the number of recorded pixels. It helps compare models, but it cannot predict by itself what you’ll read in real footage. Motion, reflections, headlights, exposure, windshield condition, and camera angle can all change the result. HDR and newer sensors are useful features, not a guarantee that plates will be readable.

  • Front and rear: the A229 Plus 2CH records 2K on both cameras, but its maximum combined mode is 60 fps front / 30 fps rear. HDR on both is 30 fps.
  • Night driving: the A229 Pro adds a larger 4K front sensor; the A510-1 offers front HDR but its rear camera is 1080p without HDR. These specifications don't establish which records the most readable plate in a particular scene.
  • Cabin recording: the A229 Plus 3CH adds a 1080p infrared view and more files to store; consider passenger notice and privacy.
  • Windshield reflections: an optional CPL can reduce dashboard reflections. Check compatibility and whether the filter is actually in your bundle; a front filter does not cover the rear camera.
  • 4K: increases potential front detail and storage use; it does not replace correct exposure or guarantee readable plates.

A229 Plus or A229 Pro?

For front/rear coverage, both have HDR on both channels, buffered parking options, a supercapacitor, optional HK4 power, and microSD support up to 512GB. Choose the Plus 2CH for 2K front + 2K rear on IMX675 sensors. Choose the Pro 2CH when the 4K IMX678 front camera is the reason you are upgrading; its rear stays 2K. Both max out at 30 fps in HDR. The Pro's front CPL filter is listed as included, while the Plus's is optional; check the exact regional box contents.

Which camera fits your car?

For a city car, a single front camera can be easier to install and less intrusive. In estates, SUVs, and vans, consider cable routing, available length, and rear-window type; make sure the camera does not obstruct your view. For work or passenger transport, choose three channels only if you really need an interior view.

Before mounting a camera, check that it does not obstruct your view or driver-assistance sensors. If you are not comfortable removing trim or working near vehicle wiring, ask a qualified installer.
